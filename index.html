WHO WE ARE

Rooted in Eastern Powell County

Middlefork Volunteer Fire and Rescue is an independent, non-profit emergency service provider staffed 100% by volunteer firefighters, officers, rescue specialists, and support members who live and work right here in the community.

Neighbors Protecting Neighbors Since 1992

Middlefork Volunteer Fire and Rescue was formally established in 1992 by a committed group of local residents dedicated to bringing localized emergency fire protection to the rural hollows and scenic mountain corridors of eastern Powell County.

In 1993, through community fundraising, volunteer labor, and local support, our permanent fire station was erected at 9219 Campton Road, Slade, Kentucky. Located approximately two miles west of Nada, this facility remains the operational headquarters and training hub for Middlefork Fire/Rescue.

Our response district is one of the most geographically diverse and visited regions in the Commonwealth. Encompassing Slade, Bowen, Nada, Natural Bridge State Resort Park, and the rugged gateway to the Red River Gorge Geological Area, our volunteers protect thousands of full-time residents and millions of seasonal tourists, hikers, rock climbers, and campers every year.

Inter-Agency & Mutual Aid Partners

Emergency incidents in the mountains demand seamless teamwork. Middlefork Fire/Rescue works side-by-side with regional partners to guarantee fast, coordinated mutual aid:

100% Volunteer Organization

Every firefighter, driver, officer, and administrative responder gives their time without receiving a salary. All funding directly supports life-saving equipment, turnout gear, fuel, and apparatus maintenance.

All-Hazards Emergency Response

We are equipped and certified to handle structure fires, motor vehicle crashes with heavy extrication, wildland & forest fires, off-road UTV accidents, and medical first-response incidents.

Certified Emergency Helipad

Middlefork Fire hosts a dedicated, certified Emergency Helipad for rapid aeromedical helicopter landings, dramatically slashing trauma transfer times from the Red River Gorge to Level-1 Trauma Centers in Lexington.

Community Education & School Safety

We work actively with local Powell County schools, civic groups, and campgrounds to present fire safety programs, smoke alarm installations, and wilderness fire prevention clinics.

OUR CAPABILITIES

All-Hazards Emergency Operations

From high-speed Mountain Parkway vehicle collisions to steep Appalachian cliffside rescues and structure fires in non-hydrant hollows, Middlefork Fire/Rescue is ready for any challenge.

Structural Fire Suppression

Rapid response attack teams for residential homes, cabins, vacation rentals, commercial lodging, and barns. Operating two structural engines with preconnected attack lines, thermal imaging, and positive pressure ventilation.

  • Interior search and rescue operations
  • Rural water shuttle & portable drop tanks
  • Thermal imaging camera scene diagnostics
  • Chimney and electrical fire suppression

Vehicle Extrication & Highway Rescue

Primary heavy extrication unit along the Bert T. Combs Mountain Parkway (KY-9000), KY-11, and winding scenic routes. Equipped with hydraulic cutters, spreaders (Jaws of Life), and vehicle stabilization struts.

  • Hydraulic cutters, spreaders & rams
  • Highway traffic management & scene blocking
  • Rollover stabilization & glass management
  • Hazardous fluids containment & mitigation

Wildland & Brush Firefighting

Specialized initial attack on woodland fires, ridge-top brush blazes, and forest fires bordering the Daniel Boone National Forest. Utilizing 4x4 Brush apparatus, forestry hose, and wildland hand tools.

  • High-clearance 4x4 wildland attack unit
  • Forestry fire line digging & containment
  • Wildland-Urban Interface (WUI) structure defense
  • Coordination with KY Division of Forestry

Red River Gorge Search & Rescue

Lead agency support for search and rescue missions within Natural Bridge State Resort Park and eastern Powell County's portion of the Red River Gorge Geological Area, working alongside Powell County SAR.

  • Backcountry trail patient carry-outs & stokes baskets
  • High-angle cliff assistance & rope rigging support
  • Lost hiker grid searches & wilderness tracking
  • Night operations with portable high-output scene lights

Certified Emergency Helipad

Middlefork Fire operates an on-site, state-certified emergency landing zone (LZ) for aeromedical evacuation helicopters (Air Evac Lifeteam, PHI Air Medical, UK Kentucky Children's Hospital).

  • Direct rendezvous point for Powell County EMS
  • Lighted and designated safe mountain landing pad
  • Fast-tracks critical trauma patients to Lexington centers
  • Available 24 hours a day in clear flight conditions

Off-Road & UTV Incident Response

Eastern Powell County contains extensive rugged logging trails, recreational off-road vehicle routes, and steep slopes. Our volunteers deploy specialized gear to reach remote wilderness accident locations.

  • Off-camber vehicle recovery & winch operations
  • Remote mountain medical stabilization
  • GPS coordinate mapping & trail reconnaissance
  • All-terrain extrication and wilderness transport

COMMUNITY SAFETY & REGULATIONS

Kentucky Forest Fire Hazard Laws

Under Kentucky Revised Statute (KRS) 149.370, seasonal outdoor burning restrictions are strictly enforced across Powell County to prevent devastating forest and wildland fires.

SPRING FIRE HAZARD SEASON February 15 – April 30
BURNING RESTRICTED: 6 AM – 6 PM

During the spring season, dead winter vegetation, fallen leaves, and rising winds create extreme fire propagation hazards during daylight hours.

  • No outdoor burning within 150 ft of woodland between 6:00 AM and 6:00 PM
  • Burning after 6:00 PM is permissible only if winds are calm and fire is attended
  • Keep water source and hand tools immediately adjacent
FALL FIRE HAZARD SEASON October 1 – December 15
BURNING RESTRICTED: 6 AM – 6 PM

Autumn leaf-drop combined with low humidity and drying autumn winds significantly elevates wildland fire danger in our Appalachian forests.

  • No outdoor burning within 150 ft of brush/woods between 6:00 AM and 6:00 PM
  • All fires must be completely cold to the touch before leaving
  • Never burn household garbage, plastics, treated lumber, or tires
YEAR-ROUND GUIDELINES Every Day Across Powell County
SAFE PRACTICES AT ALL TIMES

Regardless of season, every person lighting an outdoor fire is legally and civilly responsible for any damage caused if that fire escapes containment.

  • Always monitor weather for High Wind or Red Flag warnings
  • Campers in the Gorge: use designated campfire rings only
  • Never ignite fires during county-wide or state drought burn bans

Essential Fire Safety Advice for Residents & Cabin Owners

Creating Defensible Space Around Mountain Cabins

Because many homes and rental cabins in Slade and Bowen are nestled against forested ridges, maintaining defensible space is critical:

  • Clear pine needles, dried leaves, and debris from roofs, gutters, and decks.
  • Store firewood piles at least 30 feet away from exterior walls and porches.
  • Prune tree branches overhanging roofs or chimneys to a clearance of 10 feet.
  • Ensure driveway clearance of at least 14 feet wide and 14 feet high for fire engines.

Smoke Detectors & Home Escape Plans

A functioning smoke alarm doubles your chances of surviving a residential structure fire:

  • Install smoke alarms on every level, outside bedrooms, and inside sleeping areas.
  • Test batteries on the first of every month and replace alarms after 10 years.
  • Practice two separate escape routes from every room with your family.
  • Post visible, reflective house numbers at the entrance of your driveway for responders.

Answer the Call: Become a Volunteer

Middlefork Fire/Rescue is 100% volunteer. When the pager sounds in eastern Powell County, it's ordinary men and women doing extraordinary work for their neighbors. No prior experience is needed — we provide full training and gear.

Roles for Every Skill & Background

You do not have to run into burning buildings to make an immense impact. It takes a complete team to operate a volunteer fire and rescue department:

Structural & Wildland Firefighters

Interior and exterior fire suppression, attack hose handling, ventilation, forest fire containment, and search operations.

Apparatus Drivers & Pump Operators

Operating our engines, tanker, and brush units, driving safely on mountain routes, drafting water, and managing pressure gauges.

Rescue Technicians & First Responders

Hydraulic extrication (Jaws of Life), CPR/First Aid medical first response, wilderness litter carry-outs, and Gorge search operations.

Support, Rehab & Auxiliary Members

Incident scene rehab (water/hydration/cooling), station maintenance, community fundraising, public relations, and grant writing.

Basic Volunteer Qualifications:

  • ✓ At least 18 years of age (Junior program available for ages 16-17)
  • ✓ Valid driver's license and clean background
  • ✓ Resident of Powell County or immediate surrounding area
  • ✓ Commitment to attend regular department drills and training

Kentucky Forest Fire Hazard Seasons (KRS 149.370)

Current Status: Calculating...

Kentucky law imposes strict outdoor burning restrictions during designated Forest Fire Hazard Seasons to protect lives, homes, and public forests like the Daniel Boone National Forest.

Spring Season: February 15 through April 30
Fall Season: October 1 through December 15
Daily Restriction: Unlawful to burn within 150 feet of any woodland, brushland, or field containing dry grass between 6:00 AM and 6:00 PM.
Permitted Hours: Burning is permitted only between 6:00 PM and 6:00 AM, provided the fire is constantly attended and winds are calm.

Important Fire Safety Rules:

  • Never leave an outdoor fire unattended until completely extinguished and cold.
  • Never burn plastics, household trash, asphalt, or treated construction debris.
  • Campers in the Red River Gorge must observe specific USFS campfire regulations.
  • Always check for county-wide burn bans during hot, dry summer or autumn droughts.
